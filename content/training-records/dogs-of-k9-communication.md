My dogs are my resume. Everything I teach, I train on my own dogs first. These videos show Griz, my 1 year old working line German Shepherd, along with my 11 year old husky rescue and my kids. Each one lists the service it comes from, so you can see what that training looks like before you book it.

## Training with Kids

### Kids handling big dogs

{{< youtube 1piLf035sk8 >}}

My 6 year old Norwegian Viking child walks Griz on leash, takes the leash off, plays fetch, and gets a drop, a heel, and a down out of her. The question to ask yourself: can your six-year-old do that with a working line German Shepherd?

**Service:** [Training with Kids](/services/training-with-kids/).

## Food and Play Training

Four of these videos are [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/): behaviors taught with rewards, so the dog wants to do them.

### Training through play

{{< youtube nDTvE199qhM >}}

This is how Griz and I play. She runs through turns, heel positions, parade, center, boost, and backing up, and to her it's all a game. Food and play is the best way to teach precise movement like a [focused heel](/services/food-and-play-training/#heeling), and it's why she looks excited to work.

**Service:** [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/), advanced [obedience](/services/food-and-play-training/#obedience) and [heeling](/services/food-and-play-training/#heeling).

### Trailing games

{{< youtube 6xY6tbOXQx8 >}}

Retriever trials run double and triple blinds, where the dog is sent to bumpers it can't see. This is the search and rescue version. I walk two red balls out into a field, one past a road crossing. Red is hard for a dog to see, so Griz has to use her nose. Then I spin her around and cast her to each ball, one at a time. It's a game, and it's also impulse control, direction at a distance, and nosework in one exercise.

**Services:** [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/) builds the drive and the retrieve. We play nosework games like this in [Group Lessons](/services/group-lessons/).

### Tracking at 12 months

{{< youtube ytUBuQI52XQ >}}

Competition-style tracking with a dog who is still too young to compete. Griz follows a track footstep by footstep with her nose down. It's a lot of mental work for a dog, and if you finish with fetch, it exercises body and mind together. Any dog can learn it, and it's a good outlet for a dog with more energy than a walk can burn.

**Services:** [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/) is how tracking is taught: food in the footsteps first. Tracking is part of nosework in [Group Lessons](/services/group-lessons/).

### Finding a lost kid

{{< youtube V2qxq1B5FCA >}}

Griz at 1 year old, trailing her kid through the woods. This shows she could find her kid if he got lost in the woods. To her it's a game, and the reward at the end is one of her favorite people. To me it's practice for the day one of my kids really does wander off.

**Services:** [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/), with the find as the reward. It's also a good look at how I include kids, which is what [Training with Kids](/services/training-with-kids/) is for.

## Escape and Avoidance Training

### Stress training

{{< youtube SnMAGTHxG2Y >}}

Four sessions of force fetch, start to finish. Griz learns to *escape* pressure by taking the object, then to *avoid* it by taking the object right away. She has learned this way before, so she picks it up fast. The point isn't the fetch. It's teaching a dog to handle stress and to listen when told, even when there's no reward on the table.

**Service:** [Escape and Avoidance Training](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/). See [how I use pressure](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/#how-i-use-pressure) for the rules that keep it safe.

## E-Collar Training

### Off-leash walk by a lake

{{< youtube cFSE0V6iDTQ >}}

A long walk in the woods with two dogs and no leashes. Both dogs wear e-collars and I have the remote ready, but I never use it in this video. That's what e-collar training is for: the dogs get to run, sniff, and explore without a leash. No training is happening here. This is the payoff.

**Service:** [E-Collar Training](/services/e-collar-bootcamp/), the safe way to ditch the leash.
