As Griz hits 10 months, here are a few videos to show how she's doing.

## Working with kids

Griz is a high-energy working line German Shepherd, which means she can be hard to handle. Cassius is 5, and he's going to have a hard time managing a dog like her without some training for both of them. Once a day Cass works with either Griz or Denali.

Leash skills are the hardest for a little guy, so when we work on them I have him choke up on the lead near the collar, we use a pinch or slip collar, and I take the tail end. I give him a chance to give corrections, and I use the tail of the lead for the ones he misses. For play, Griz is generalized on long-distance arounds, so that's an easy way for him to play fetch with her.

We work on classical conditioning together too. He's still deciding on his reward marker, and I'm not worried if he changes it often, because he's a kid. Our dogs are smart enough to pick up on the other classically conditioned signs anyway. If I give Cass food and ask him to work on his reward marker, the dogs are already tuned in.

## Trailing

We're still doing tracking, and it's not really recommended to do both tracking and trailing, but I'm keeping it up anyway. When I trail with search and rescue folks, they use longer distances with higher-value rewards at the end. Checking every footstep is exhausting for Griz, so I don't enforce it there. When I set up tracking, we do shorter distances with smaller rewards. Here's some trailing setup before her first search and rescue training session.

## Reactivity

During her fear period, at 4-5 months, Griz was bitten by a couple of dogs. That caused fear-based reactivity toward off-leash dogs. This video is boring because we're catching it early, as one should, but I'm putting it here anyway.

The idea with this kind of reactivity is to bring her to the edge of her comfort zone without crossing it. At first I just had her in the trees where she could lie down, chill, and watch dogs go by. Then we graduated to walking the trail with other dogs. That was harder, but adding obedience made a lot of progress. When she's focused on me for a heel, she worries less about the rest of the world, so I use that.
