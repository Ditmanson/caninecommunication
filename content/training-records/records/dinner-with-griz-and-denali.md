You can feed your dog kibble. A lot of people do, and their dogs live long, full lives. I had one amazing dog who couldn't digest kibble, which sent us down a long road of raw food.

We've done it a lot of ways. At times we saved money by getting livers, hearts, kidneys and the rest from farmers. That turned out to be gross for me and gross for the dogs. Yeah, dogs don't like the way organs taste. I've checked with about 5 dogs, and 5 out of 5 didn't like them. They'd eat it if they were hungry enough, but they'd pass if they weren't starving.

Then I moved to premade raw grinds for a while, and that was OK. But if you watch documentaries, even wolves eat berries, grass, and other non-meat stuff, and kibble already has fruit, veggies, and supplements. So we went looking for non-meat ways to get the micronutrients. You can make your own and save money, but I lack time, so I use Dr. Harvey's.

The instructions say to mix it with hot water, meat, and oil and serve. If I do that, Denali won't eat it. So for dinner, when Denali is eating, I mix it with an egg instead of hot water. In the morning I just mix it with hot water and some meat for Griz. She'll eat anything.

The dogs also get tons of treats, and depending on what I'm eating, I usually share: blueberries, carrots, and yogurt through the week. Griz gets most of the table scraps to beef up on calories, but if we have something super healthy like salmon and broccoli, both dogs get some, and I cut some of Denali's food ahead of time.
