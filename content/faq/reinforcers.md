A reinforcer is anything that makes a behavior more likely to happen again. I split them into two kinds.

**Primary reinforcers** are biological needs for survival: food, water, warmth, and sex. Your dog doesn't have to learn to want them.

**Secondary reinforcers** are the behaviors dogs use to get those needs: chasing, tug, nose work, and the like. They're rewarding because, for a dog, they've always led to a primary reinforcer. Once your dog learns a game like nose work, the game rewards itself.

*Note:* psychology textbooks often use "secondary reinforcer" for a learned reward like a clicker. I call those markers. See [What are verbal markers?](/faq/verbal-markers/)

## Using them in training

To build food drive:

- Reduce caloric intake
- Feed once a day
- Measure food
- Use higher-value treats
- Pair food with secondary reinforcers

To build toy drive, start with food rewards, then add chase and frustration games.

In [Group Lessons](/services/group-lessons/), we chain these together with the [Premack principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premack%27s_principle), so each activity rewards the one before it.
