A verbal marker is a word that tells your dog exactly what's coming next. On its own, *yes* means nothing to a dog. Through [classical conditioning](https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/mcat/behavior/learning-slug/v/classical-conditioning-neutral-conditioned-and-unconditioned-stimuli-and-responses), we pair it with a reward until the word itself creates the same excitement as the reward. Markers work like a [clicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clicker_training), but they also cover corrections, and you never leave them at home.

## The markers I use

| Marker | Type | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| *Yessss* | Termination | Behavior is done; the reward is coming |
| *Goooood* | Continuation | Keep doing that; a reward may come |
| *Uh-uhhh* | Correction | Wrong choice; you're about to lose the reward |
| *No* | Punisher | Stop; a correction is coming |

## Why a calm *no* works

Every marker gets stronger each time it predicts its outcome. Once *no* reliably predicts a correction, a calm *no* works better than any amount of yelling, and marked corrections stay effective at much lower intensity.

## Commands are markers too

Once your dog knows the basic markers, commands like *sit* and *down* get their meaning from them. This is [higher-order conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_conditioning). See [Verbal Commands](/services/verbal-commands/).

Markers are taught in [Verbal Marker Training](/services/verbal-markers/), the foundation for everything else.
