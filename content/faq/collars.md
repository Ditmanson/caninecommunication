No. Pinch collars and e-collars are the clearest communication tools for teaching [escape and avoidance](/faq/escape-and-avoidance/) skills. They let your dog experience an uncomfortable consequence safely, instead of the dangerous consequences the real world applies to unacceptable behavior.

We never train with aversives without showing your dog an alternative they can do to earn positive reinforcement. By pairing these tools with play and food, your dog doesn't see them as cruel. If you're using these tools for the first time with me, we'll condition them so your dog sees them as a source of fun, food, and freedom.

## Denali's story

My rescued Husky, Denali, taught me that force free training has its limits. I used e-collars and light aversive training with her for a couple of years before she became the well-behaved dog she is now. Today I can trust her with no collar in almost any environment.
