Traditional training, also called escape and avoidance training, teaches a dog to start or stop a behavior to get away from light, unpleasant pressure.

- **Escape learning:** the dog learns a behavior that ends the pressure. Leash pressure guides your dog into a sit; the moment they sit, the pressure releases.
- **[Avoidance learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avoidance_learning):** the dog learns to do the behavior before the pressure ever arrives. Eventually, the command alone is enough.

## Leash pressure

Leash pressure is how I teach escape and avoidance. We use it to guide your dog forward, backward, into a sit, a down, or onto a place, and the release of pressure is the reward. Pairing it with a correction marker means less pressure over time.

## Is it safe?

Yes, when it's done right. Two rules matter most:

- **The pressure must always be escapable.** Pressure the dog can't escape leads to fear and [learned helplessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_helplessness).
- **Start low.** We begin below the level that changes behavior and raise it only until we see results.

I don't use hitting, alpha rolls, yelling, or social pressure. Those are unclear, too intense, and give the dog no way out.
