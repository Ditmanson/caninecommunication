## Sessions

Training sessions are **$100 each**. Sessions are designed to run about an hour, sometimes a little longer or shorter. You're charged by the session, not by the hour. Because every plan is built around your dog, the total depends on which services you need. For example, [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/) takes one or two sessions, and a full [Fundamentals](/services/fundamentals/) build is about seven.

[Group Lessons](/services/group-lessons/) are $30 per dog. [E-Collar Training](/services/e-collar-bootcamp/) is billed per session, plus the cost of the collar.

## The consult deposit

Every client starts with an [In-Home Consult](/services/in-home-consult/), booked with a **$30 deposit**. The In-Home Consult runs about 30 minutes. Optional video or phone consults beforehand are free. The deposit:

- is paid at the consult
- is credited toward your first training session if you book within 60 days
- is non-refundable
- is waived if I decide at the consult that I can't help your dog

## Equipment

None of this is required. It's the gear I use and recommend. At the consult, we'll go over what you already have and what would help. Some gear you own may be the wrong size.

- **Treat pouch:** I used to use pockets, but a dedicated pouch is a game changer.
- **6"–3' leash:** Light enough that your dog can wear it with a pinch collar without accidental discomfort.
- **8'–30' leash:** Long enough for recalls and freedom on enrichment walks.
- **Herm Sprenger [pinch collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_collar#Aversive_collars):** It looks mean, but it's a game changer. It's less likely to injure your dog than a flat collar (I'll explain at the consult), and it delivers low-intensity aversive stimulation safely while still extinguishing behaviors.
- **Harness or agitation collar:** Something your dog can pull against to build frustration without hurting their throat. Some dogs can get by with a flat collar.
- **[Muzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle_(mouth_guard)):** Muzzle training is good for dogs even if they aren't a danger to anyone. It's an activity we can use to teach your dog how to deal with stress, and it gives you an additional tool you can use right now, while we get past whatever your dog needs to get past.
    - For some small breeds, like pugs, a muzzle is overkill. In those cases an [Elizabethan collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethan_collar) works: it gives us the same controlled practice on gear you're more likely to use.

## Equipment buyback

Equipment I buy for you can be sold back to me at 90% of its value when training ends. That gear goes to future clients who need to save money. Do the math: if I buy back the same piece of gear 10 times, some lucky handler gets it free.
