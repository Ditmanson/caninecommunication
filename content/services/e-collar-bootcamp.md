## Why the E-collar?

We want to maintain the ability to provide pressure and/or corrections to our dog, but we don't want to carry a leash. An [electronic collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_collar) lets us do that.

## Two methods

There are two different philosophies we can embrace here. In method 1, we have a *collar-dumb* dog, meaning the dog doesn't know the collar is providing the corrections. In method 2, we have a *collar-savvy* dog. We can always transition from method 1 to method 2, but we cannot transition from method 2 back to method 1.

### Method 1

In method 1, we use the collar sparingly. The consequence is that we can solve some problems quickly and permanently, and remove the collar earlier in our dog's development. In this method the e-collar is used strictly for [positive punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punishment_(psychology)).

We go out of our way to prevent the dog from learning what the collar or remote is. We use it for things like jumping up on people, counter surfing, digging, chewing on things, and other unwanted behaviors. We can try using it for recall; however, doing so significantly increases our chances of ending up with a collar-savvy dog. In this method we use the collar as little as possible, at high levels of stimulation.

### Method 2

The e-collar's usefulness expands greatly if we are willing to have a collar-savvy dog. If we let the dog understand the purpose of the collar, then we can use it for negative reinforcement as well as positive punishment. We can incorporate it into any of our training, which is where we find ourselves with some extra sessions. We will use it with negative reinforcement for recall, heeling, barking, and reactivity.

Because our goal has shifted from keeping the dog collar-dumb to using the collar as a primary rule enforcer, we get to switch from high stim to low stim. In this method we keep our collars turned down to 10-25, depending on the dog. I greatly favor this method, but it's important to be conscious that we can never turn a collar-savvy dog back into a collar-dumb dog.
