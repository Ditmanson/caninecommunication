## Video/Phone Consult

The point here is to determine whether I'm a good fit as your dog trainer. We'll discuss the general ways to accomplish your goals. At the end, I'll ask you to schedule an In-Home Consult, where I can get the specific information I need to build your training plan.

## What happens at the In-Home Consult

I meet you and your dog where you live. We talk through what's going on, what you want out of training, and which services fit. Then we [make your plan](#how-we-make-your-plan). Most dogs start with [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/), then [Escape and Avoidance Training](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/), the foundation everything else builds on. Expect it to take about 30 minutes.

For dangerously reactive dogs, we'll discuss how many desensitization sessions are needed before training can start. I need to be able to work safely around you and your dog, so an overly reactive dog may need [Behavior Modification](/services/behavior-consult/) first.

## How we make your plan

Every plan is built around a stimulus and a threshold.

A **stimulus** is whatever your dog struggles to behave around. It can be a place, like your daily walk, or an event, like people coming over. Your dog can get a certain distance from that stimulus while still behaving appropriately. We call that line their **threshold**. My job is to help you move it.

{{< steps >}}
1. **Pick the stimulus.** We choose the environments and events you want your dog to behave around and through. That gives every behavior we train a goal.
2. **Find the threshold.** I watch how close your dog can get to the stimulus and still behave appropriately. That's where we start.
3. **Choose how we cross it.** We can use only [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/), only [Escape and Avoidance Training](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/), or layer both. I'll give you the pros and cons of each for your dog. For most behaviors I suggest both, but you decide with me.
4. **I write the plan.** It lists the behaviors, the sessions, and the homework that get us across the threshold.
5. **You approve it.** Then we get started.
{{< /steps >}}

### What a layered plan looks like

A layered plan typically goes like this:

1. **Food and play first.** We teach the behavior somewhere quiet, well inside your dog's threshold. This [takes the novelty out](/services/food-and-play-training/#why-we-use-it) of the behavior without stress.
2. **Then escape and avoidance.** We make the behavior reliable, so it holds [even when something more exciting than you shows up](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/#why-we-use-it).
3. **Then we move toward the stimulus.** We take both into the real world and close the distance a little at a time, leaning on whichever your dog needs that day.

The [Fundamentals Bootcamp](/services/fundamentals/) is an example of a full layered plan.
