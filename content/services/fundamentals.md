# Day 1 Classic Conditioning and Reinforcer Markers
Day 1 is all about classical conditioning. Way back in 1904, a scientist by the name of Ivan Pavlov, was studying the digestion in dogs. He was awarded the noble price for discovering that a neutral stimulus can gain the same properties as a primary or secondary reinforcer through close association in space and time. 
For us, our neutral stimulus is going to be a verbal marker, _yes_ or _no_, and these verbal markers will take the properties of ether a positive or negative stimulus. This gives power to our verbal markers to create an involuntary response in the dogs. 

On this first day our markers are going to be reinforcing markers and impulse control markers. Each time we use the markers, the association gets stronger, and the emotions we can bring out in our dogs also gets stronger. As time goes on we use this with operant conditioning to reinforce behaviors. 

This day is all force free, meaning no use of aversive stimuli is used

# Day 2 Operant Conditioning and Punisher markers through leash pressure

Training with aversives is usually a hard selling point, it gets a bad reputation due to a strong misunderstanding of their use and intensity. We do not encourage hitting, alpha doging, yelling, or social pressure. These kind of punishers are usually unclear, to intense, and provide no means of escape and avoidance for the dog. I consider all of these, to include yelling, inhumane. However, the world is full of aversives, and proper use of punishers are usually the fastest means to solving the problem you've come to me with as well as poetentially life saving for the dog.

We are going to discuss the 4 corners of operant conditioning in detail on this day, as well as the importance of escape/avoidance training. Escape/avoidance training is the old school traditional dog training method. This means the dog learns to start/stop behaviors by escaping an unwanted pressure and eventually avoiding the unwanted pressure completely. 

Doing this properly early, means we have to do less of it in the future. These lessons last longer than force free methods and work faster; the caviot is too much pressure, or unescapable pressures can result in extremely fearful dogs. We will avoid that by starting with an intesnity that is not high enough to shape behavior, and add intensity until we see results. It's much  easier than it sounds and I'll be there to demonstrate with my dogs as well. 

You'll also notice with my dogs, the pressure that is used is extremely light. This is because I've classicly conditioned my markers in the past and the association with my correction markers has already been made. Similar to the reinforcer markers, the more a punisher marker is used, the stronger the effect it has. This means a calm _no_ can have sigificantly higher effects than a screaming _f*$ STOP FIDO!_ 

# Day 3 Higher order Conditioning aka verbal commands

Commands are actually verbal markers. We call them higher order markers because we instill properties in them with our reinforer/punisher markers. A "_down_" is a command to down, but it's also a continuation marker. Meaning if the dog stays in the down they will either be occasionally rewarded, or they stay in the down to **avoid** our negative reinforcement. For this day we are going to focus on the fundamental commands that every handler needs. 
- Sit
- Down
- Place
- Here
- We can add/subtract commands based on your needs.

The other thing we'll be working on this day is engagement between you and your dog and ignoring distractions. These distractions will likely just be me, but if the dog's ready then we'll also head to a more stimulating envorinment, where the environment itself starts distracting your dog.

# Day 5 Heeling Training

Heeling is actually a ton of fun. For some reason, we have it in our heads, that this is hard work, it's not. This is actually a really cool skill to work on with your dog that solves all sorts of problems. You're probably thinking oh it will solve my leash pulling, and yeah, I suppose hat's true, but we already have the tools from day 2 to solve that issue. 
What heeling gives us, is an extremely dynamic skill where we can engage with our dogs and always improve. This can be used to solve reactivity issues, walk safely near traffic, grab our dogs attention so they don't engage with other dogs or the environment, charge our markers, increase our bond, and always give us a bench mark upon which we can improve. 
We can heel in the house from one room to another, before dinner, out in the real world, or any number of other situations, and it can always be improved. 

We have:
    - Casual heeling
    - Contact heeling
    - focused heeling
    - Heeling while running
    - heeling while riding a bike
    - heeling while driving a car

There's just so many reasons to train heeling, it's nothing else, it's going to improve your bond with your dog
