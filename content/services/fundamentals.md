## Day 1: Classical Conditioning and Reinforcer Markers

Day 1 is all about [classical conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_conditioning). Way back in 1904, a scientist by the name of [Ivan Pavlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov) won the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine) for his work on digestion in dogs. Along the way he discovered that a neutral stimulus can gain the same properties as a primary or secondary reinforcer through close association in space and time.

For us, the neutral stimulus is going to be a verbal marker, *yes* or *no*, and these verbal markers will take on the properties of either a positive or negative stimulus. This gives our verbal markers the power to create an involuntary response in the dogs.

On this first day our markers are going to be reinforcing markers and impulse control markers. Each time we use the markers, the association gets stronger, and so do the emotions we can bring out in our dogs. As time goes on, we use this with operant conditioning to reinforce behaviors.

This day is all force-free, meaning no aversive stimuli are used.

## Day 2: Operant Conditioning and Punisher Markers Through Leash Pressure

Training with aversives is usually a hard sell. It gets a bad reputation due to a strong misunderstanding of their use and intensity. We do not encourage hitting, [alpha-rolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_roll), yelling, or social pressure. These kinds of punishers are usually unclear, too intense, and provide no means of escape or avoidance for the dog. I consider all of these, including yelling, inhumane. However, the world is full of aversives, and proper use of punishers is usually the fastest way to solve the problem you've come to me with, as well as potentially life-saving for the dog.

We are going to discuss the four quadrants of [operant conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operant_conditioning) in detail on this day, as well as the importance of escape/avoidance training. Escape/avoidance training is the old-school traditional dog training method. The dog learns to start or stop behaviors by escaping an unwanted pressure and eventually avoiding that pressure completely.

Doing this properly early means we have to do less of it in the future. These lessons last longer than force-free methods and work faster; the caveat is that too much pressure, or inescapable pressure, can result in extremely fearful dogs. We avoid that by starting with an intensity that is not high enough to shape behavior and adding intensity until we see results. It's much easier than it sounds, and I'll be there to demonstrate with my own dogs as well.

You'll also notice that with my dogs, the pressure used is extremely light. This is because I've classically conditioned my markers in the past, so the association with my correction markers has already been made. Similar to the reinforcer markers, the more a punisher marker is used, the stronger its effect. This means a calm *no* can have significantly higher effects than a screaming "f\*$# STOP FIDO!"

## Day 3: Higher Order Conditioning, aka Verbal Commands

Commands are actually verbal markers. We call them [higher order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_conditioning) markers because we instill properties in them with our reinforcer/punisher markers. "*Down*" is a command to down, but it's also a continuation marker. That means if the dog stays in the down, they will either be occasionally rewarded or stay in the down to **avoid** our negative reinforcement. For this day we are going to focus on the fundamental commands that every handler needs:

- Sit
- Down
- Place
- Here
- We can add or subtract commands based on your needs.

The other thing we'll be working on this day is engagement between you and your dog, and ignoring distractions. These distractions will likely just be me, but if the dog is ready, we'll also head to a more stimulating environment where the environment itself starts distracting your dog.

## Day 4: Chill Training

Our discussions on this day will be light and focused on reward schedules. Our goal today is to flatten the dog, meaning reduce excitement, or teach a dog to chill even in a high state of arousal. We're going to add distance and duration to our commands from last time. We'll go over strategies for how to fade help, whether it's with the leash or luring, as well as how to fade the place box, because while helpful, it's not always going to be available in the real world.

## Day 5: Heeling Training

Heeling is actually a ton of fun. For some reason we have it in our heads that this is hard work. It's not. This is a really cool skill to work on with your dog that solves all sorts of problems. You're probably thinking, "Oh, it will solve my leash pulling," and yeah, I suppose that's true, but we already have the tools from day 2 to solve that issue.

What heeling gives us is an extremely dynamic skill where we can engage with our dogs and always improve. It can be used to solve reactivity issues, walk safely near traffic, grab our dog's attention so they don't engage with other dogs or the environment, charge our markers, increase our bond, and always give us a benchmark on which to improve.

We can heel in the house from one room to another, before dinner, out in the real world, or in any number of other situations, and it can always be improved.

We have:

- Casual heeling
- Contact heeling
- Focused heeling
- Heeling while running
- Heeling while riding a bike
- Heeling while driving a car

There are just so many reasons to train heeling. If nothing else, it's going to improve your bond with your dog.

This day will be mostly practicals, with very little discussion.

## Day 6: Recall Training

#### "Recall is the most important skill a dog needs. Why do we wait until the last day for this?"

Well, we haven't. We've been working on engagement through distractions this entire time. We've been putting in the work to make this part easy and fun. Today isn't really the day we teach it; today is the day we bulletproof it.

We have a variety of games and skills to play today. We will use lots of positive reinforcement on this day; however, it is important that we get into an environment that is stimulating enough to require some use of negative reinforcement or correction. The last thing either of us wants is for your dog to fail to recall in a dangerous situation. The aversives we use are controlled and will not harm your dog; the aversives the world uses to enforce a recall could be fatal. So we need to do this correctly.

## Day 7: Capstone

The [AKC Canine Good Citizen](https://www.akc.org/products-services/training-programs/canine-good-citizen/) program and [IGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzhund) BH certifications are out of my reach. I'm not planning to become a certifier for either, because at K9-Communication we have a stronger belief in training records than in certifications. A certification is a way to prove what you know. The Canine Good Citizen is a good certification, but it doesn't meet your dog where they are. It could be too hard or too easy for your dog at this point. We want to show where you and your dog currently are, so we favor training records over certifications.

Together we will design a capstone that showcases how well trained your dog is. The better you did with your homework, the better this capstone can be. We want the environment to be as stimulating as your dog is ready for. We want their movements to be as fast and precise as they are ready for.

Common things we want to show:

- **Enrichment walking** - *How do we handle leash pulling?*
- **Impulse control** - *How does our dog go through a doorway, or wait for permission to eat food?*
- **Chillin'** - *How long will our dog stay on a place box, in a down, or in a sit without rewards or corrections? If we still need rewards or corrections, what does our reward schedule look like, and how often do we need to reapply pressure?*
- **Heeling** - *How good is our heel? Can we use it to engage with our dog in highly distracting environments? Can we use it to keep our dog safe in otherwise unsafe conditions?*
- **Recall** - *SPEED! SPEED! SPEED! How fast does our dog recall, and how strong can our distractions be while we still get a bulletproof recall?*
