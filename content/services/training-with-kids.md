A dog that listens to you doesn't automatically listen to your kids. Dogs don't generalize well: a *sit* learned from you belongs to you until someone else practices it too. Kids are also smaller, louder, and faster than adults, which makes them more exciting to a dog and easier to ignore.

In these sessions, your kid does the handling and I coach. You stay with us the whole time.

## Why it comes after the foundation

Your dog needs to know the behaviors before your kid asks for them. [Food and Play Training](/services/food-and-play-training/) gives your dog the markers and commands, and makes working with people fun. [Escape and Avoidance Training](/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/) makes those commands reliable, so they hold when a kid's timing is off or the reward is late. Then we hand the leash to your kid.

Adults handle the corrections. Kids handle the fun: rewards, play, and clear commands.

## What we train

### Markers and commands

Your kid learns the same [verbal markers](/faq/verbal-markers/) you use, said the same way. Consistency across the family is what makes a dog reliable. We practice *sit*, *down*, *place*, and *here* until your dog answers your kid the first time.

### Walking on leash

How to hold the leash, where the dog should be, and what to do when the dog pulls or spots a squirrel. We start somewhere quiet and add distractions as the team gets better.

### Play with rules

Fetch is the easiest way for a kid to bond with a dog, and it's full of training: wait for the throw, bring it back, [drop it](/services/food-and-play-training/#obedience), do something to earn the next throw. Play with rules burns your dog's energy and teaches your kid that they control the game.

### Being safe around dogs

Your kid learns to read a dog: what a stiff tail or a hard stare means, when to give a dog space, and what to do if a dog gets too excited. This applies to every dog they'll meet, not just yours.

## What to expect

- **An adult stays the whole time.** These sessions aren't childcare, and you'll need to keep the practice going at home.
- **Short sessions inside the hour.** Kids and dogs both learn best in short bursts, so we work, play, and work again.
- **Homework for the family.** A few minutes a day of your kid working your dog does more than any single session.
- **A plan that fits your kid.** What's realistic depends on your kid's age and your dog. We'll talk about it at the [In-Home Consult](/services/in-home-consult/).

If your dog has bitten, or guards food, toys, or space from your kids, we start with [Behavior Modification](/services/behavior-consult/) instead. See also [Does my dog's bite history matter?](/faq/bite-history/)

## Whole family welcome

I'd love to have the [whole family](/faq/family-multiple-dogs/) at any session. This service is for when you want your kids to be handlers themselves, not just in the room.
