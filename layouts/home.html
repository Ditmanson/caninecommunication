A complete training system.

Dogs don't learn English. They learn to associate some words with outcomes based on the predictable nature of these words. The words "good" or "bad" only hold meaning if they carry a predict what's going to happen next. Maybe god predicts love and praise, and bad predicts leash pressure or losing their toy. It's only when the word predicts an outcome, does it establish meaning to the dog. There is an art to teaching this that is not completely intuitive. Often we make tiny mistakes and use words with our dogs and they predict an outcome we didn't desire.

Our communication system isn't built on English, it's built on creating predictive associations with a word/sound and our next actions. This is called Classical Conditioning.

The dog can't speak English to us. They need to learn that actions they exhibit also have predictable outcomes. They learn being in the kitchen means free snacks, or coming when called gets play time and so they learn to hang out in the kitchen at dinner time, or to come when called. Likewise they learn that cat's run when approached and so they learn to chase cats. They learn that when they bark, it has an effect on their environment and so they bark more.

The other half of our communication system is built on establishing a clear consequence to behaviors we want to encourage or extinguish. This is called Operant Conditioning.

All dog training; force free, traditional, balanced, or accidental — traces back to two kinds of conditioning. Master both, and you can create or extinguish any behavior your dog is (or isn’t) offering.

Classical conditioning Creates an involuntary response to a neutral stimulus — the control of emotional responses through verbal markers. Operant conditioning Shapes behavior through consequences.
